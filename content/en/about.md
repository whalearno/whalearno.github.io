I am Chih-Ming Wang, a graduate of the Institute of Physics at National Tsing Hua University in Taiwan. My academic background is primarily in optics and laser physics, with research involving photonic crystals, ultrafast lasers, and terahertz radiation.

In my younger years, I continued into a doctoral program at National Tsing Hua University, but later left my studies for personal reasons and began my professional career.

Over the years, my work has spanned optoelectronics, embedded systems, industrial automation, energy management, and artificial intelligence. I currently work in Taiwan on the research and development of industrial systems and AI technologies.

These years of engineering experience have given me opportunities to approach problems from different perspectives: from physical phenomena to mathematical models, from sensor data to system behavior, and from theoretical analysis back to validation in the real world.

## Reconnecting with Physics

Even after leaving academic research, I never lost my interest in physics.

However, as work, family, and everyday responsibilities gradually occupied more of my time, the physics I once loved became something I could only follow occasionally through science news and articles online.

For a long time, I believed that, after so many years away from academia, I might never have the opportunity to engage deeply with physics again.

Physics continued to advance, and many new theories and mathematical tools had emerged far beyond what I had learned during my university years. Having been away from campus for so long, finding a suitable starting point—or someone to turn to for guidance—no longer seemed easy.

Physics gradually became a world I still loved, but could only observe from the outside.

Then, with the rapid development of artificial intelligence in recent years, something began to change.

With AI, I no longer have to stop at reading science news. I can explore the physical principles behind new research, study scientific papers, and revisit mathematical concepts that were once familiar but now require a deeper understanding.

When I encounter something I do not understand, I can ask questions repeatedly. When I discover an interesting mathematical relationship, I can follow it further. When a possible connection between different fields comes to mind, I can explore it through discussion.

None of these activities was entirely impossible before. But for an engineer who had spent many years away from academia while balancing a demanding professional life, the barriers were often considerable.

AI has lowered many of those barriers.

Of course, AI cannot guarantee that every answer is correct, nor can it replace rigorous mathematical proofs, physical verification, or genuine academic exchange. Nevertheless, it offers a way of learning and exploring that would have been difficult to imagine not so long ago.

To me, the most meaningful aspect of artificial intelligence is not simply its ability to improve productivity or perform increasingly complex tasks.

**It is that AI has given me the opportunity to reconnect with a dream I once had to leave behind.**

This time, I do not necessarily need to return to university, nor do I need to set a particular academic achievement as my goal.

Simply being able to think deeply about mathematics and physics again, and to explore questions that genuinely interest me, is already a source of great joy.

## My Research Interests

I particularly enjoy starting from simple and fundamental mathematical relationships and gradually exploring the structures that may underlie more complex phenomena.

Sometimes, a mathematical expression that initially appears straightforward can reveal unexpected properties when reconsidered in terms of dimensionality, symmetry, constraints, or geometric meaning. Whether these structures have broader significance, and whether they can be applied across different fields, are questions that continue to fascinate me.

My current interests include mathematical and geometric structures, number theory and mathematical analysis, theoretical physics, artificial intelligence, and machine learning.

I am also interested in the mathematical structures that different disciplines may have in common. Such connections do not always lead to new results, but the process of exploring them is fascinating in itself.

My engineering background has also shaped the way I approach theoretical questions. I often think about mathematical models in relation to real systems, and about how abstract structures might connect to observable physical phenomena.

For me, these explorations are not another obligation added to my daily work. On the contrary, they are a way to relax, maintain my curiosity, and rediscover the pleasure of learning and discovery.

## Why I Created This Website

As I began exploring mathematics and physics again, I gradually accumulated notes, mathematical derivations, observations, and ideas.

Some have developed into more complete discussions. Others remain exploratory, while some are simply questions that I find worth pursuing.

I wanted a simple place where I could organize these thoughts, share them openly, and continue maintaining them over time.

This website is therefore not primarily a showcase of academic publications. It is more like a collection of public research notes that will continue to grow and evolve.

Here, I share notes on mathematics and physics, observations related to artificial intelligence and engineering, and occasional essays about scientific methods, knowledge, and ideas.

Some articles may be revised as my understanding develops. Earlier arguments may be corrected, reconsidered, or even abandoned.

I hope to preserve the paths through which these ideas develop, while making a clear distinction between established mathematical results, verifiable derivations, and conjectures that remain to be tested.

## Exploration and Exchange

I believe that scientific research is not only about finding correct answers, but also about continually reconsidering how we formulate our questions.

New insights do not always emerge from elaborate theories. Sometimes, they begin with a fresh look at a simple mathematical relationship, or with asking one more "why" about an explanation we have long taken for granted.

I deeply appreciate the opportunities that AI has given me. At the same time, I recognize that meaningful research must ultimately withstand mathematical scrutiny, physical verification, and examination by the scientific community.

I am happy to share these evolving thoughts, and I welcome different perspectives, relevant references, constructive criticism, and opportunities to learn from researchers in other fields.

If these notes encourage someone to take an interest in a mathematical question, a physical phenomenon, or a scientific idea—and perhaps lead to further discussion—then this website will have served its purpose.
